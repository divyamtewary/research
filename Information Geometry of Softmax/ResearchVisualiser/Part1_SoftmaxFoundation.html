Section 1

Big Question: How does Softmax warp the geometry of representation space?

Neural network hidden states are often treated as points in a flat Euclidean space. But models don't output hidden states — they output probability distributions. Softmax is the bridge, and it bends.

Intuition — The Dog-to-Cat Line

In a flat Euclidean space, moving from "dog" to "cat" means moving in a straight line. Each step along that line smoothly transitions one concept into another. This is the Linear Representation Hypothesis — semantic concepts align along straight directions.

But here's the catch: Neural networks don't directly compare hidden vectors. They convert them into probability distributions via Softmax, and probability space is curved — not flat.

Section 2

Big Question: How do raw logits become probabilities?

Softmax does two things: exponentiation and normalization. Together they ensure outputs are positive, bounded, and sum to one — but they also introduce nonlinear distortion.

Intuition — The Auction Analogy

Imagine each token is bidding for probability mass. Exponentiation amplifies the strongest bids (making the gap between them larger), then normalization divides the total so everything sums to 100%. The richest bidder gets the most, but everyone gets something.

Intuition — Gradient as Arrow

Gradients point in the direction of steepest ascent. For $A(\lambda)$, the gradient points toward the distribution that would most increase the log-partition function — which turns out to be the probability vector itself. In other words, $A$ "encodes" the distribution in its slope.
